## Chunk-size sweep (row 21; Dev train split, 30 attacked + 30 clean, selection-level only, EXPLORATORY)

Window risk = PIGuard on previous + current + next chunk (the frozen setting). Raw risk = the chunk alone. B0 attack-in-context is 24/30 for every granularity.

| Granularity | Chunks per target email | Risk | B2 in-context / removed | Ours in-context / removed | Answer kept B2 / Ours (of 10) | Clean chunks (chars) dropped on attack cases, B2 / Ours | Clean chunks dropped on clean cases |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sentence | 4.2 | window | 1 / 23 | 1 / 23 | 3 / 5 | 29 (5,156) / 25 (4,351) | 0 |
| 1–2 sentences ≤150 chars (frozen) | 5.3 | window | 2 / 22 | 2 / 22 | 7 / 8 | 29 (3,148) / 23 (2,538) | 0 |
| ~256 tokens | 1.1 | window | 1 / 23 | 1 / 23 | 1 / 5 | 30 (11,342) / 21 (8,122) | 0 |
| ~512 tokens | 1.0 | window | 0 / 24 | 0 / 24 | 0 / 5 | 30 (13,130) / 20 (7,946) | 0 |
| any granularity | — | raw | 13 / 11 | 13 / 11 | 10 / 10 | B2 0–5 (0–250 chars), Ours 0 | B2 0–5 |

Notes on the sweep:

- Under raw risk the results are almost identical across granularities. Our construction always chunks the attack text separately (the text before, the attack and the text after BIPIA's insertion), so the attack chunk is pure attack whatever the granularity.
- At coarse granularity in a real corpus the attack would share a chunk with clean text. The coarse rows therefore understate the collateral loss a real deployment would see.
- With window risk, coarser units lose more clean text and more answers (B2 keeps 0–1 of 10 answers at ~256/512 tokens).
- The frozen 1–2-sentence setting gives the best answer retention among the window settings. Single sentences make windows narrower in content, but B2 still drops neighbouring answer sentences (3/10).
- Dev ASR is 0 for all methods, so this sweep measures selection only.
